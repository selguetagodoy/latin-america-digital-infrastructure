# Country profiles

## Brazil

Anchor market: São Paulo. Brazil is the scale leader in the benchmark. It combines the largest harmonized wholesale market in the comparison with the broadest cloud-region footprint and the highest number of active IXPs. Its main constraint is not digital scale but the complexity and territorial variation of energy connection and permitting.

## Mexico

Anchor market: Querétaro. Mexico is the benchmark's hyperscale-growth reference. Querétaro has become a major cloud and colocation cluster, while the country combines several full cloud regions with strong recent absorption. Energy carbon intensity and institutional indicators remain important counterweights in the comparison.

## Chile

Anchor market: Santiago. Chile combines a relatively dense cloud footprint, strong Internet-exchange participation and low vacancy in the harmonized market comparison. Its competitive position is closely linked to renewable energy availability, international connectivity and a comparatively strong institutional environment.

## Colombia

Anchor market: Bogotá. Colombia is the target market in the benchmark and remains smaller in wholesale data-center scale than Brazil, Mexico and Chile. It nevertheless has an active interconnection ecosystem and an operational Oracle Cloud region. Its opportunity is tied to domestic demand growth and the maturation of the Bogotá market.

## Peru

Anchor market: Lima. Peru has a notable IXP footprint and a meaningful Andean-market role, but the public evidence base does not yet support the same harmonized wholesale-MW comparison available for São Paulo, Querétaro, Santiago and Bogotá. The repository therefore keeps market-scale fields blank rather than imputing capacity.

## Panama

Anchor market: Panama City. Panama is included primarily as a logistics and international-connectivity hub. Its role is structurally different from the larger hyperscale markets: submarine connectivity, regional services and special economic regimes matter more than absolute domestic market scale.

## Costa Rica

Anchor market: San José. Costa Rica is the institutional and services reference in the benchmark. Its comparative strengths include renewable electricity, digital readiness and institutional indicators, while its local data-center market remains smaller and less transparent in harmonized MW terms.

## Argentina

Anchor market: Buenos Aires. Argentina provides a large Southern Cone demand base and a relevant regional interconnection market. Its digital infrastructure case is balanced by macroeconomic and regulatory considerations, which makes it analytically useful as a contrast with Chile and Brazil.

## Interpretation

These profiles are not rankings. The benchmark is deliberately multidimensional. A market can lead in scale while lagging in institutional quality, energy emissions or resilience. The intended use is comparative diagnosis rather than a single composite score.
