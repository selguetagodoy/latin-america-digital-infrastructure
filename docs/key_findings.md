# Key findings — 2026 regional benchmark

## 1. Brazil is in a different scale category

São Paulo records 536.7 MW of operational inventory in the harmonized market comparison, around 1.8 times Querétaro, 3.2 times Santiago and more than 12 times Bogotá. Brazil also has the broadest cloud footprint and 51 active IXPs in the country benchmark.

## 2. Mexico combines scale with rapid hyperscale expansion

Querétaro is the second-largest comparable market at 298.2 MW and records five active full cloud regions in the benchmark. Its main counterweights are a lower renewable-generation share and higher electricity carbon intensity than Brazil, Chile or Colombia.

## 3. Chile has a comparatively balanced infrastructure profile

Santiago is smaller than São Paulo and Querétaro in absolute MW, but Chile combines four active full cloud regions, one formally announced region, eight IXPs, an Internet resilience score of 64 and one of the strongest rule-of-law scores in the eight-country comparison.

## 4. Colombia remains a smaller wholesale market with room to deepen

Bogotá records 44.3 MW in the harmonized market comparison, well below the three larger markets. Colombia nevertheless combines seven active IXPs, an operational Oracle Cloud region and a renewable-generation share of 77% in the retained energy benchmark.

## 5. Peru is stronger in interconnection than its cloud footprint suggests

Peru has no retained full cloud region in the benchmark, but it records 12 active IXPs — the second-highest count among the eight countries. This illustrates why cloud presence and interconnection depth should not be collapsed into a single indicator.

## 6. Costa Rica illustrates the value of a multidimensional benchmark

Costa Rica is not a hyperscale market in the same category as Brazil, Mexico or Chile, but it performs strongly on renewable electricity, rule of law and Network Readiness. Its profile is closer to an institutional and services reference than to a regional scale leader.

## 7. Panama's role is primarily connective and logistical

Panama is included because its structural role differs from the larger domestic markets. International connectivity, logistics, regional services and special economic regimes are more relevant to its digital-infrastructure profile than absolute wholesale data-center inventory.

## 8. Argentina combines a large demand base with macro-regulatory constraints

Buenos Aires remains a relevant Southern Cone interconnection and enterprise market. Argentina records four active IXPs and 400 IXP members in the benchmark, but its investment case cannot be understood without considering macroeconomic and regulatory conditions.

## Cross-market interpretation

The benchmark does not support a single deterministic ranking. Brazil clearly leads on scale, but Chile performs relatively well across several enabling dimensions; Mexico leads a strong hyperscale expansion story; Costa Rica performs well institutionally; and Peru has a deeper IXP footprint than its cloud-region count would imply.

The analytical value of the repository is therefore in identifying combinations of strengths and constraints rather than compressing all variables into one score.

Cutoff: 2026-08-13.
